MASTER DESIGN

1. TWO TRACKED PARABOLIC TROUGHS (outdoors, in the yard)
   - Two identical trough mirrors standing side by side, about 3 m apart.
   - Each trough: a long curved mirror, 1.6 m wide and 1.9 m long, with a deep U-shaped parabolic cross-section.
     The two rims of the U are level with the receiver tube. Inner surface is mirror-bright silver; back and ribs are matte grey steel.
   - Each trough is one single curved mirror. There are NO flat side mirrors, NO extra panels, NO dish, NO lens.
   - The long axis of each trough runs north–south and is tilted about 15 degrees: one end low (about 0.9 m above the ground),
     the other end high (about 1.4 m above the ground). Two steel end posts carry each trough, one short, one tall.
   - The mirror hangs like a cradle underneath its receiver tube and rotates around that tube to follow the sun from east to west.
     A small weatherproof worm-gear drive box sits at the tall post.

2. RECEIVERS (one per trough; they never move)
   - A thin straight tube lying exactly along the focal line of each trough, about 0.4 m above the bottom of the U.
   - It is a black steel tube 42 mm thick inside a clear glass tube 70 mm thick, with small pale ceramic rings holding the black tube
     in the centre of the glass.
   - Along the top of the glass runs a narrow insulated cap, about 11 cm wide, light aluminium outside and mirror-bright underneath.
   - The receiver is 25 cm longer than the mirror at each end.
   - The receiver is fixed to the two end posts. Only the mirror turns. Rigid insulated pipes, clad in dull aluminium and about
     6 cm thick, leave the high end of each receiver and run along a low wall into the kitchen. No flexible hoses.

3. SALT HEAT STORE WITH BAKING PLATE (inside the kitchen)
   - A squat, heavily insulated cylinder about 0.85 m wide and 0.85 m tall, brushed stainless-steel skin, standing in a shallow steel tray.
   - Insulation: 200 mm of rock wool (grey fibrous material) directly around the steel salt tank. No aerogel layer.
   - Its top is a round, dark cast-iron baking plate 45 cm across, level with the kitchen counter, with a raised rim.
     A thick insulated lid with a handle covers the plate when nobody is cooking.
   - Beside the main plate, a smaller round contact pad 20 cm across for a kettle.
   - On the front, a small panel with three green lamps and one red lamp and a small temperature display.
   - The two insulated pipes from the troughs enter at the back.

4. WATER STORE
   - A white vertical cylinder, 120 litres, about 0.55 m wide and 1.2 m tall, on a low stand against the outside wall under a small roof.
   - Thin insulated pipes link it to the flat water collector, to the kitchen and to the wash area.

5. FLAT WATER COLLECTOR
   - One flat glazed panel, 1 m by 2 m, dark blue-black under glass, aluminium frame, on a fixed tilted stand in the yard,
     set slightly lower than the water store.

6. PHOTOVOLTAIC PANELS AND CONTROLLER
   - One standard photovoltaic module, about 1.1 m by 1.8 m, dark blue cells in an aluminium frame, on a fixed tilted stand next to
     the troughs. A cable runs from it to the heat store.
   - One small photovoltaic panel, about 0.5 m by 0.6 m, on a short pole.
   - A grey controller box, 30 cm by 40 cm, on the kitchen's outside wall, with a small display.

7. MODERN TWO-PLATE COOKTOP (inside the kitchen)
   - A counter-height hob with two flat, round, dark metal plates set in a stainless-steel top. One rotary knob per plate.
     No flame, no gas, no glowing elements. Two thin insulated pipes feed it from the heat store.

8. OVEN
   - A built-in insulated oven of about 40 litres beside the cooktop: stainless-steel door with a glass window and a round thermometer dial.

9. ROOM HEATING
   - One white flat panel radiator on the living-room wall, with two thin pipes coming from the water store.

10. PORTABLE HEAT CARTRIDGE
   - A flat round steel disc, 30 cm across and 12 cm thick, with two side handles and a removable insulated cover.
     Its place is on the kettle pad of the heat store.

Things that must never appear: flat mirrors beside the troughs, a satellite-dish reflector, bundles of glass vacuum tubes,
a lens over the receiver, flames, smoke, chimneys, gas bottles, flexible hoses on the receivers, text or logos.
